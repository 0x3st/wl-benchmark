# RUBRIC — Argument Writing
*Adapted from the official CUHKSZ composition rubric (RUBIC1.docx). Same dimensions, weights, level bands and deduction rules; descriptors are specified for argumentative writing.*

**Task**: an argument essay on **why we could use smartphones while walking inside the campus**, delivered bilingually in ONE markdown document — the English version first, then a faithful Chinese (中文) version. Must include **at least three evidences** supporting the claim and **a rebuttal paragraph** criticizing the claim “走路不看手机” (don't look at your phone while walking), in both versions. Well structured, solidly supported. **The RUBRIC grades the English version.**

## Score bands (per dimension)

| Level | Label | 25-pt dims (IC / ORG) | 50-pt dim (LANG) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-range | Exemplary | 25–20 | 50–40 |
| B-range | Convincing | 19–17 | 39–34 |
| C-range | Acceptable | 16–14 | 33–28 |
| D-range | Needs improvement | 13 | 27–25 |
| F-range | Not satisfactory | 12–0 | 24–0 |

## 1. INTELLECTUAL CONTENT / THINKING (25)

Understands the material; main points support the topic; no main points missing; no factual errors. Interesting, relevant theme; keeps focus; main points clear. The relationship between the details and the main idea is made clear. Good examples and specific supporting details.

| Level | Descriptors (argument-specific) |
|---|---|
| Exemplary | ≥3 distinct, concrete evidences (campus scenarios, plausible data, authorities, analogies), each explicitly tied back to the thesis; rebuttal paragraph genuinely engages “走路不看手机” (safety concern) and rebuts it with reasoning/evidence rather than dismissal; no factual errors; CUHKSZ-specific grounding where sensible. |
| Convincing | 3 evidences present and relevant; rebuttal exists and addresses the counter-claim, though somewhat brief; minor factual looseness. |
| Acceptable | Only 2 solid evidences or evidence is generic/repetitive; rebuttal is present but shallow (assertion without reasoning); focus drifts occasionally. |
| Needs improvement | ≤1 real evidence; rebuttal missing or merely restates the thesis; noticeable factual errors. |
| Not satisfactory | No identifiable argument; evidences absent; off-topic. |

## 2. ORGANIZATION (25)

Paper level: strong introduction; strong conclusion; logical and convincing progression of ideas; consistent and appropriate point-of-view. Paragraph level: effective transitions connecting paragraphs if needed; paragraph boundaries recognized; internal paragraph coherence maintained. Other: indents; clean markdown structure.

| Level | Descriptors |
|---|---|
| Exemplary | Thesis stated precisely in the introduction; each evidence gets its own coherent paragraph with a topic sentence; rebuttal placed strategically (before the conclusion) with a smooth pivot; conclusion synthesizes rather than repeats. |
| Convincing | Clear intro-body-conclusion; paragraph order logical; transitions mostly effective; rebuttal positioned reasonably. |
| Acceptable | Structure recognizable but paragraphing weak (multiple ideas crammed, or one-sentence paragraphs); transitions mechanical ("Firstly/Secondly") without logical glue. |
| Needs improvement | No clear thesis or paragraphs lack boundaries; rebuttal bolted on randomly; ideas ordered by accident. |
| Not satisfactory | No essay structure; disconnected sentences. |

## 3. LANGUAGE USE (50)

**Basic**: Fragment; run-on; sentence length; SVO; word order/location; missing word; wordy. Verb tense; SV agreement; verb phrases; active verbs; singular/plural; spelling. Articles; prepositions; pronouns; conjunctions; adjectives; adverbs. Capitalization; punctuation; spacing; meaning unclear; awkward phrase; repetitious. Fronted adverbial conjunctions; absolutes; clichés; slang; hyperbole; profanity.
**Advanced**: qualifying language; possessive vs. prepositional phrases; style; voice.
**Vocabulary**: limited vocabulary; incorrect word form; incorrect word use; poor word choice.

| Level | Descriptors |
|---|---|
| Exemplary | Near error-free across the Basic checklist; advanced control: hedged claims where evidence is probabilistic ("may", "tends to"), argumentative voice without slang/hyperbole/profanity; precise connectives (concession, cause, contrast) beyond and/but/so. |
| Convincing | Few basic errors; claims mostly well qualified; vocabulary adequate, occasional poor word choice. |
| Acceptable | Recurring basic errors but meaning intact; absolutes and over-claiming ("everyone knows", "always"); repetitious wording. |
| Needs improvement | Dense basic errors; wordy or awkward phrasing throughout; over-claiming and informal register. |
| Not satisfactory | Errors obscure meaning; no argumentative register. |

## Total (100 points max) — Deductions

Start from IC + ORG + LANG, then deduct:

- Instructions followed: fewer than 3 evidences → −10; rebuttal paragraph absent → −10; the essay argues the opposite claim → −25.
- Length (English version): task length requirement (if given) out of range → −3 per 100 words over/under.
- Bilingual delivery: Chinese (中文) version missing, a stub, or machine-literal → −10.
- Language: English version missing or not in English → total capped at 50.
- Structure: no essay form (bullet list only / chat reply) → total capped at 54.
- Other (explain in summary).

**Grade mapping**: A = 86, A− = 80, B+ = 76, B = 72, B− = 68, C+ = 64, C = 60, C− = 56, D+ = 53, D = 50, F = <50.

## Deductions (applied on top of the dimensions above)

- Hard constraint violations (word/character ranges, required headings, evidence tags, boundary paragraphs — see the task sheet): **-5 per violated constraint**. The review sheet lists machine-checked results per constraint; verify FAIL entries before deducting.
